# New User Onboarding Guide

## Step 1 — Get an account

Your PI, or anyone in your lab, raises an onboarding request with your full name, email
address, and the lab you are joining. The request requires the PI's acknowledgement
before it can be submitted; it is not possible to onboard a user into a lab without it.

The request creates a pending action. Nothing is created until the facility admin
approves it, and both the request and the approval are recorded in the audit trail.

## Step 2 — Receive your account code

Your PI assigns you at least one account code. Every booking you make must carry one.
If you work across two labs you may hold codes for both, but a given booking charges
exactly one code.

## Step 3 — Complete Biosafety Level 2

New users are enrolled in Biosafety Level 2 automatically. It runs fortnightly and is a
prerequisite for any work involving human or primary animal material.

## Step 4 — Request instrument training

Instrument training is separate and must be requested for each instrument you intend to
book. See the training requirements document for validity periods. Book at least three
weeks ahead: hands-on sessions are capped at four trainees.

## Step 5 — Make your first booking

Once training is recorded, the instrument becomes bookable. Check availability first,
then book the slot with your account code. Remember the 24-hour cancellation window.

## What to expect in your first month

- Badge access is issued within two working days of approval.
- Your first invoice arrives in the first week of the following month.
- If you have not used your badge for 90 days it deactivates automatically, so book
  something in your first three months even if only a short induction session.

## Who to ask

Anything about training, access, or billing goes to the core facility admin through the
request system. Scientific questions about experimental design go to your PI first — the
facility advises on instrument capability, not on your experiment.
