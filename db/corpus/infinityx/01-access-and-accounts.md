# Facility Access and User Accounts Policy

Owner: Core Facility Administration · Reviewed annually · Applies to all three cores

## 1. Scope

This policy governs who may hold an account on the facility management system, how an
account is obtained, what each account type may do, and when an account is suspended or
closed. It applies to the Advanced Imaging Core, the Genomics Core and the Mass
Spectrometry Core, and to every internal, external and commercial user of those cores.

It does not govern instrument competence. Holding an account lets you see the facility;
it does not by itself let you book anything. Instrument access is governed by the
Instrument Access Levels and Training Policy.

## 2. Account types

| Account type | Who holds it | What it can do |
|---|---|---|
| User | Researchers, students, technicians | Book instruments they are certified on, raise service requests, submit samples, view their own charges |
| Group Leader (PI) | Principal investigators | Everything a User can do, plus approve group members, attach account codes, approve invoices for their group, view all activity within their lab |
| Core Staff | Facility scientists and technicians | Operate instruments, run service requests, record maintenance, adjust bookings on behalf of users |
| Core Manager | Facility management | Rates, calendars, user approval, invoice commitment, reporting across their core |
| Administrator | Systems and finance | Cross-core configuration, financial exports, audit access |

An account has exactly one type. A person who is both a PI and a hands-on user holds a
single Group Leader account; the extra rights are additive, not a second login.

## 3. Obtaining an account

### 3.1 Internal users

1. Complete the online registration, giving your institutional email address, department
   and the lab you are joining. Personal email addresses are not accepted.
2. Nominate your Group Leader. The request is routed to them automatically.
3. The Group Leader acknowledges the request and attaches at least one account code that
   your work may be charged against.
4. Core administration performs a final check and activates the account.

The account is not usable until step 4 completes. Steps 2 and 3 are the ones that
usually stall: a request waiting on a Group Leader who is travelling can sit for a week,
and the system will not chase them more than twice.

### 3.2 External academic users

External academic users follow the same route with two additions. A named internal
sponsor must be recorded against the account, and an inter-institutional agreement must
be in place before any charge can be raised. Where no agreement exists, the account is
created in a view-only state so that the user can see availability and rates while the
agreement is negotiated.

### 3.3 Commercial users

Commercial users are onboarded by core administration rather than by self-registration.
A purchase order or a signed rate agreement must exist before activation. Commercial
accounts are always attached to a commercial rate card and cannot be attached to an
internal account code.

## 4. One person, one account

Accounts are personal. Sharing a login, working under a colleague's account, or leaving a
session open for someone else to use are all treated as the same offence, because the
consequence is the same: the usage record and the charge attach to the wrong person, and
the audit trail becomes fiction.

A first incident results in a written warning to the user and their Group Leader. A
second results in suspension of the account for fourteen days. This is the only sanction
in this policy applied automatically rather than at a manager's discretion, because the
integrity of the usage record underpins every charge the facility raises.

## 5. Account codes

An account code is the financial identifier that work is charged against. It belongs to a
lab, not to a person. Every account carries at least one; many carry several — a lab
running work under two funding sources will normally hold one code per source.

Rules that apply without exception:

- A booking must carry an account code at the moment it is created.
- The code must belong to a lab the user is a member of. You cannot charge another lab.
- A Group Leader may add or remove codes from their group's members at any time. Removing
  a code does not alter charges already raised against it.
- Where a user holds more than one code, the system offers their most recently used code
  as the default. The user remains responsible for choosing correctly; a charge raised
  against the wrong code is corrected by the dispute process, not by deletion.

## 6. Changes of circumstance

| Change | What you must do | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Joining a second lab | Ask the second Group Leader to add you | Both memberships are held; codes from both become selectable |
| Leaving a lab | Nothing; the Group Leader removes you | Access to that lab's codes ends immediately, past charges are unaffected |
| Changing institution | Register again as an external user | The internal account is closed, not transferred |
| Extended absence | Nothing | See section 7 |

## 7. Dormancy and closure

An account with no activity for one hundred and eighty days is marked dormant. A dormant
account cannot make a booking until it is reactivated, which the holder can do themselves
by signing in and confirming their lab and account codes are still correct.

An account is closed when the holder leaves the institution, when their Group Leader
requests closure, or after three hundred and sixty-five days of dormancy. Closure removes
the ability to sign in. It does not delete the usage history, the charges, or the samples
associated with the account, all of which are retained under the relevant retention
schedules.

## 8. Access outside staffed hours

The cores are bookable between 08:00 and 20:00 UTC, seven days a week. Access to the
building and the instrument rooms outside 09:00–17:00 on weekdays requires a current
out-of-hours authorisation, which is granted by the facility manager and reviewed every
twelve months.

Out-of-hours authorisation is granted per person, not per lab, and is contingent on
holding Level 1 access on at least one instrument. It is withdrawn automatically if that
Level 1 access lapses.

Badge access is deactivated after ninety days of inactivity. Reactivation is a request to
core administration and is normally same-day.

## 9. What core staff may see

Core staff and managers can see all bookings, usage records, service requests and samples
within their own core, because they are responsible for operating it. They cannot see
another lab's private documents, another user's uploads, or the content of research data
held in the cloud storage area.

Group Leaders can see all activity within their own lab, including bookings and charges
made by their group members. Group members cannot see each other's charges.

## 10. Questions

Account and access questions go to core administration. Questions about who may use a
particular instrument go to the core that owns it. Questions about whether a charge is
correct go through the dispute process in the Recharge and Invoice Approval Policy, not
through account administration.
