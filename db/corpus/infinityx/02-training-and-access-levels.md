# Instrument Access Levels and Training Policy

Owner: Core Facility Training Lead · Reviewed annually

## 1. Why levels exist

Instruments in the cores range from a benchtop analyser worth the price of a car to a
cryo-electron microscope worth the price of a building. The competence needed to run them
safely differs by orders of magnitude, and so does the cost of a mistake. Rather than a
single "trained / not trained" flag, every user holds a level on every instrument they
may touch, and the booking system enforces it.

A level is held **per instrument**, not per person and not per core. Being Level 1 on the
Confocal C2 says nothing about your access to the Cryo-EM Titan.

## 2. The three levels

### Level 3 — trainee

You are learning the instrument. A member of core staff is physically present for the
whole session. You may not book the instrument yourself; staff book on your behalf and
the session is recorded against your account so that your training history builds.

Most users reach the end of Level 3 after two or three sessions. Some instruments require
more; the Cryo-EM Titan requires a minimum of five supervised sessions regardless of prior
experience elsewhere.

### Level 2 — supervised independent

You operate the instrument yourself, but only while core staff are on site — 09:00 to
17:00 on weekdays. You may book your own sessions within those hours. If you hit a
problem, staff are down the corridor.

Level 2 is not a waiting room. It exists because most errors that damage an instrument or
a sample happen in the first dozen unsupervised sessions, and the cost of having someone
to ask is far lower than the cost of the alternative.

### Level 1 — independent

You may book and operate the instrument at any time the facility is open, including
outside staffed hours if you also hold an out-of-hours authorisation. This is the level
most regular users reach and stay at.

## 3. Moving between levels

| From | To | Requirement |
|---|---|---|
| — | Level 3 | Active account, safety induction complete, Group Leader's acknowledgement |
| Level 3 | Level 2 | Staff sign-off after the required supervised sessions, plus a short practical check |
| Level 2 | Level 1 | At least five independent sessions at Level 2, no unresolved incidents, staff sign-off |
| Any | Suspended | An incident, a lapsed certification, or six months without use |

Progression is never automatic and never time-served alone. A user who has completed
eleven Level 2 sessions but has twice left the instrument in a state that needed staff
intervention does not advance, and the reason is recorded.

## 4. Currency and lapsing

Instrument certification lapses after twelve months without a recorded session on that
instrument. This is not a punishment; it reflects that an instrument you have not touched
in a year is an instrument you no longer know.

A lapse of less than sixty days is cleared with a refresher session, typically half an
hour with staff. Beyond sixty days the initial training must be repeated in full.

Safety and compliance certifications have their own validity periods, which are recorded
in the training requirements document and are separate from instrument competence. Both
must be current for a booking to be accepted.

## 5. What the booking system enforces

The scheduler refuses a booking outright when:

- the user holds no level on that instrument;
- the user holds Level 3, which cannot self-book;
- the user holds Level 2 and the requested slot falls outside staffed hours;
- any required certification has lapsed;
- the instrument is out of service for maintenance.

The refusal names which condition failed. It does not offer to book anyway and flag it,
because a booking that requires a human to notice a warning is a booking that will
eventually be made by someone who did not notice.

## 6. Training records

Every training event is recorded against the user: the instrument, the level reached, the
staff member who signed it off, and the date. A user can see their own record at any time.
Their Group Leader can see the records of everyone in their lab. Core staff can see the
records for the instruments they are responsible for.

The record is what the system checks. If your record says Level 2 and you believe you were
signed off to Level 1, the record governs until it is corrected — raise it with the core
that owns the instrument rather than attempting to book.

## 7. Group Leader responsibilities

A Group Leader acknowledging a training request is confirming three things: that the
person is a member of their group, that the work is within the group's remit, and that the
group will meet the charges arising. It is not a statement about competence, which is the
core's judgement alone.

A Group Leader may withdraw a group member's access at any time, for any reason, without
giving one. The withdrawal takes effect immediately and cancels that user's future
bookings on the affected instruments.

## 8. Incidents

An incident is any event where an instrument, a sample or a person was at risk. Report it
the same day through the technical issue route, whether or not anything was damaged.

Reporting an incident is not a disciplinary process. Failing to report one is. A user who
reports a mistake keeps their level in almost every case; a user who leaves a problem for
the next person to discover does not.

## 9. Training on request

Core staff run scheduled training rounds for the high-demand instruments each month. For
everything else, training is arranged on request; raise a service request naming the
instrument and your intended application, and the core will schedule it.

Training sessions are charged at the instrument's normal hourly rate unless the core has
designated the instrument as training-exempt. The exempt list is published in the
instrument catalogue.
