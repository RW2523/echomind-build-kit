# Publications, Acknowledgement and Authorship Policy

Owner: Core Facility Directorate · Reviewed annually

## 1. Why the facility asks

The cores are funded on the basis of the research they enable. A paper that used a core
instrument and says so is, in a very direct sense, what pays for the next instrument. A
paper that used one and does not say so is invisible to that case.

This policy sets out what the facility asks for, what it does not, and how the line between
acknowledgement and authorship is drawn.

## 2. Acknowledgement — the default

Work that used facility instruments or facility services should acknowledge the core by
name. The form of words is yours; the facility does not supply a mandated sentence, because
a mandated sentence is one that gets copied without thought.

What the acknowledgement needs to contain is the core's name and, where the work depended on
a specific instrument, that instrument. "Imaging was performed at the Advanced Imaging Core
on the Confocal C2" is enough.

Where an instrument was funded by a named award, the award should be acknowledged too. The
instrument catalogue records which instruments carry that requirement.

## 3. Authorship

Acknowledgement is the default; authorship is the exception, and the facility does not ask
for it as a matter of course.

Core staff qualify for authorship when they have made an intellectual contribution to the
work — designing the approach, developing a method for the study, or interpreting results —
judged against the same criteria the journal applies to any other author. Operating an
instrument competently, however skilfully, is a service and is acknowledged rather than
authored.

Where staff believe they qualify, they raise it with the corresponding author early, not at
submission. Where a user is unsure, ask the core manager; the facility would far rather have
that conversation than have a paper appear with a contribution it should have declared.

The facility never asks for authorship as a condition of service, and no user should feel
that declining changes the service they receive.

## 4. Recording a publication

Tell the core when work is accepted. Give the citation or DOI, the cores involved, and the
instruments if you can.

This can be done through a service request or by replying to the annual request the facility
sends out. Both routes reach the same record.

## 5. What the facility does with the record

Publication records are used for three things: the annual report, funding and renewal cases,
and demonstrating impact to the institution.

They are also used to tell users about relevant work — with permission, and only within the
institution. The facility does not publish a list of who is working on what, because a
publication record is a map of a research programme and some of it is not yet public.

## 6. Pre-publication confidentiality

Everything the facility knows about your unpublished work is confidential. Core staff do not
discuss one lab's work with another, and the facility does not use unpublished work in
promotional material, case studies or demonstrations without written consent.

Where two labs are visibly working on similar problems, the facility says nothing to either.
Staff who find themselves in that position raise it with the core manager rather than
deciding alone.

## 7. Data and figures

The facility does not claim rights over data collected on its instruments. The data belongs
to the lab that produced it.

Where the facility wishes to use an image for a report, a poster or teaching, it asks first,
every time, and takes no for an answer without asking why.

## 8. Corrections and retractions

Where a question is raised about data collected in a core, the facility will provide, on
request from the corresponding author or the institution's research integrity office, the
instrument's service and calibration history for the relevant dates, the session records,
and the acquisition settings as they were recorded.

The facility provides this factually and takes no position on the underlying question. The
asset audit trail is designed for exactly this: it cannot be edited after the fact, which is
what makes it worth having.

## 9. What the facility asks of you, in one line

Say which core and which instrument, tell us when it is accepted, and ask us early if you
think a staff member should be an author.
