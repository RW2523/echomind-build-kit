# Actual Usage Tracking and Reconciliation Policy

Owner: Core Facility Management · Reviewed annually

## 1. Two numbers

The facility records two independent numbers for every instrument:

- **Scheduled hours** — time that was booked.
- **Tracked hours** — time the instrument itself logged as in use, from its own controller
  or from the room's access system.

They are collected by different mechanisms and neither is derived from the other. That is
the point: a single number can be wrong without anyone noticing, whereas two numbers that
disagree are an invitation to look.

## 2. What each is used for

Scheduled hours are what you are charged for. That rule is set in the Scheduling and
Cancellation Policy and this policy does not change it.

Tracked hours are used for three things: to find usage that was never booked, to inform
capacity planning, and to give a user evidence when they dispute a charge.

## 3. Reconciliation

The two records are reconciled monthly, before invoices are issued. Three outcomes matter:

### Tracked usage with no booking

Someone used the instrument without reserving it. This is walk-up use, and it is charged
to the user's default account code at the normal rate.

Walk-up use is not forbidden — an instrument standing idle and a researcher with a sample
that will not wait is a situation with an obvious right answer — but it is charged, and a
pattern of it prompts a conversation, because it makes the calendar untrustworthy for
everyone else.

Where the tracked session cannot be attributed to a user at all, the time is recorded
against the instrument as unattributed and is not charged to anyone. Unattributed time
above 5% of an instrument's monthly usage is investigated.

### Booked time never used

The booking is charged in full, per the charging basis. It is also counted towards the
no-show record if there was no activity whatsoever.

### Persistent divergence

A user whose tracked hours are consistently far below their scheduled hours is
over-booking, and is wasting their own budget as much as the facility's capacity. A user
whose tracked hours consistently exceed their scheduled hours is running past the end of
their slot, which affects whoever booked next.

Either pattern, sustained over three months, produces a note to the user and their Group
Leader with the figures. It is informational; no charge follows from the pattern itself.

## 4. What tracked hours are not

Tracked hours are not a measure of productivity, and the facility does not report them as
one. An instrument logging four hours says nothing about whether those four hours produced
anything, and the facility will not answer questions framed that way — including from
Group Leaders about their own group members.

Aggregate tracked hours are reported per lab and per instrument for capacity planning.
Per-user tracked hours are visible to the user, their Group Leader and core staff.

## 5. Gaps in tracking

Not every instrument reports usage. Where an instrument has no tracking, only scheduled
hours exist, and the reconciliation is skipped. The instrument catalogue records which
instruments are tracked.

Where a tracking feed fails, the gap is recorded and that instrument is excluded from that
month's reconciliation rather than being treated as zero usage. A failed feed producing
apparent "no usage" for a busy instrument would otherwise generate a month of nonsense.

## 6. Using this in a dispute

When you dispute a charge, the facility compares the booking record against the tracked
usage log and shows you both. If the tracked log shows the instrument was in use for the
period charged, that is strong evidence the booking was used. If it shows nothing, the
charge still stands under the charging basis — but a pattern of it is exactly what section
3 is for.

## 7. Retention

Tracked usage records are retained for three years, which spans the dispute window several
times over and supports year-on-year capacity planning. They are not research data and are
not subject to the research data retention schedule.
