# Recharge, Billing and Invoice Approval Policy

Owner: Core Facility Administration and Research Finance · Reviewed annually each July

## 1. What this covers

How a charge comes into existence, how it becomes a line on an invoice, who approves that
invoice, how a disagreement is resolved, and what happens when nobody acts. It covers
instrument time, service requests, consumables and staff time across all three cores.

## 2. How a charge arises

A charge is created by exactly one of four events:

| Event | Charged as | When it lands |
|---|---|---|
| An instrument booking | Booked hours × instrument rate | At session end |
| A service request completing | Staff hours and any per-sample fee | On completion |
| A consumable issued from the stockroom | Unit price × quantity | At issue |
| Tracked usage with no matching booking | Observed hours × instrument rate | At monthly reconciliation |

Nothing else creates a charge. In particular, holding an account, being a member of a lab,
and having an instrument certification are all free.

## 3. Rates

Internal rates are published per instrument in the instrument catalogue and are reviewed
annually each July. External academic users pay 1.4× the internal rate. Commercial users
pay 1.8× the internal rate.

Rates do not change mid-period. An invoice for a given month uses the rate that was in
force on the first day of that month, so a July increase never reaches a June invoice.

Staff time on service requests is charged at a single facility-wide rate rather than per
person, so that the cost of a piece of work does not depend on who happened to be free.

## 4. The invoice lifecycle

Invoices are issued monthly, in arrears, in the first week of the following month. Each
invoice covers one account code for one calendar month, and its total is the sum of its
lines. A line corresponds to a chargeable item: instrument time, a service request, or a
consumable.

An invoice moves through three states:

### Review

The invoice is visible to the Group Leader who owns the account code. Lines can be queried
and, if the query is upheld, corrected. Nothing has yet been sent to the institution's
financial system.

The review period is fourteen days from issue. The core manager can extend it for a
specific invoice on request, and does so routinely over the summer.

### Commit

At the end of the review period the invoice commits automatically, whether or not anyone
looked at it. Committing exports the invoice to the institution's financial system and
posts the charge against the account code.

Automatic commitment is the part of this policy that surprises people, so it is stated
plainly: **silence is agreement**. An invoice nobody reviews commits exactly as if it had
been approved line by line.

### Posted

The charge is in the financial system. Corrections after this point are credit notes
raised against a later invoice, not edits to the original, because the original has already
been reported.

## 5. Disputing a line

Raise a dispute within sixty days of the invoice date. A dispute names the invoice period,
the account code, and the specific line — not "the invoice looks too high", which cannot be
investigated.

The facility compares the booking record against the instrument's tracked usage log and
responds within five working days. Outcomes are:

- **Upheld** — the line is removed or corrected. If the invoice is still in review, it is
  amended; if it has committed, a credit note is raised.
- **Upheld in part** — most commonly where a session overran and the overrun was genuine.
- **Not upheld** — the charge stands, with the evidence attached to the response.

Disputes are free and carry no penalty. A user who disputes often and is usually right is
telling the facility something useful about its own records.

## 6. What is not disputable

- Booked time that was not used. This is the charging basis, not an error. See the
  Scheduling and Cancellation Policy.
- A charge against the wrong account code where the user chose the code. The remedy is a
  transfer between codes, which the Group Leader can request, not a removal.
- Rates. A rate can be queried before a period begins; it cannot be disputed after use.

## 7. Budgets and thresholds

A Group Leader may set a monthly budget against any account code they own. The system
does not block work when a budget is exceeded — a hard stop mid-experiment would waste
more money than it saves — but it notifies the Group Leader at 80% and again at 100%.

Where a code is exhausted or closed by the institution, bookings against it are refused at
the point of booking, with the reason given.

## 8. Cross-lab and multi-core work

Work that spans cores produces one invoice line per core, on the same invoice, because the
account code is the unit of billing rather than the core.

Work funded jointly by two labs is split by the Group Leaders in advance, as a percentage
recorded against the project. The facility does not split charges retrospectively, because
the record of who agreed to what no longer exists by the time an invoice appears.

## 9. Credits and write-offs

The core manager may credit a charge without a dispute where the facility is at fault:
instrument failure, a booking the facility cancelled, a sample lost in the core's custody,
or a training session incorrectly charged on an exempt instrument.

Write-offs above a threshold set by research finance require a second approval. The
threshold and the approver are held in the financial configuration rather than in this
policy, so that changing them does not require a policy revision.

## 10. Records

Every invoice, every line, every dispute and every credit is retained for seven years, as
required by the institution's financial record-keeping rules. This retention is independent
of research data retention and is not affected by a user's account being closed.
