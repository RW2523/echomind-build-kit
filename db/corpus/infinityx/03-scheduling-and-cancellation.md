# Scheduling Operations Guide

Owner: Core Facility Management · Reviewed annually

## 1. What this guide covers, and what it does not

This guide covers the mechanics of using the calendar: how far ahead you can book, how to
change a booking, what a no-show is, who may book for someone else, and what happens when
an instrument fails mid-session.

It does **not** restate the booking rules themselves. Session length limits, fair-share
caps, the cancellation notice period and the charge for a late cancellation, instrument
status and bumping are all set out in **Booking and Cancellation Rules**, which is the
single authority on them. Where this guide and that document appear to differ, that
document governs.

That separation is deliberate. A rule written down twice is a rule that will eventually
say two different things, and the version a user happens to find first is the version they
will follow.

## 2. The calendar is the record

An arrangement made verbally with a member of staff is not a booking until it appears in
the calendar. Staff who agree to something at the bench will make the booking there and
then, or ask you to; if neither happened, you do not have the slot.

This matters most for the instruments with contention. A user who believes they have an
arrangement, and a user who has a booking, will both turn up.

## 3. How far ahead

Slots open on a rolling fortnight: a given day becomes bookable at 00:00 UTC fourteen days
before it.

The rolling window exists so that nobody can claim a month of prime time in January. It
also means the calendar looks empty further out than two weeks, which is not the same as
the instrument being free.

Where work genuinely needs longer planning — a visiting collaborator, a time-course that
must start on a fixed date, teaching — ask the core manager, who can place a booking
outside the window.

## 4. Changing a booking

| What you want | When you can do it | What it costs |
|---|---|---|
| Shorten it | Any time before it starts | The released time follows the ordinary cancellation rules |
| Move it | Any time before it starts | Treated as a cancellation plus a new booking |
| Extend it | Any time, into free time immediately after | Nothing extra beyond the added time |
| End it early | During the session | Nothing is refunded; the instrument shows free for the next user |

Ending a session early is worth doing even though it saves you nothing. The next user sees
the instrument is available, and the tracked-usage record reflects what actually happened.

Once a session has started it cannot be shortened retroactively.

## 5. No-shows

A no-show is a booking where the instrument recorded no activity at all and which was not
cancelled. It is charged in full and it is recorded against the user.

Three no-shows within a rolling ninety days prompts a conversation with the core manager,
and may result in a temporary reduction in how far ahead that user can book. The purpose
is not punishment: a user who repeatedly reserves time they do not use is denying it to
others, and the calendar stops being a useful signal of what is actually free.

Illness, instrument failure and genuine emergencies are not no-shows. Tell the core and the
record is corrected.

## 6. Booking for someone else

- **Core staff** may book on behalf of any user, and routinely do so for trainees who are
  not yet allowed to self-book. The booking is recorded against the user and charged to
  the user's account code, not the staff member's.
- **A Group Leader** may book on behalf of a member of their own group.
- **Nobody else** may book on behalf of anyone, including a colleague at the next bench
  with a deadline.

## 7. Recurring bookings

Available to fully independent users on request, up to eight occurrences, reviewed each
quarter.

A recurring booking with a pattern of no-shows is cancelled in its entirety rather than
occurrence by occurrence, because the pattern rather than any single miss is the problem.

## 8. Registering interest

Where an instrument is fully booked you can register interest in a period. When a slot in
that period is released — by a cancellation or a shortened session — everyone who
registered is notified at the same moment and it goes to whoever claims it first.

Registering interest is not a queue and does not reserve anything.

## 9. When an instrument fails during your session

1. Stop, and do not attempt a fix or a restart. The error state is often the most useful
   diagnostic available and restarting discards it.
2. End the session so the instrument does not continue to show as in use.
3. Raise a technical issue immediately, with the time and what you were doing.

Charges for the affected time are removed once the fault is confirmed. You do not need to
dispute the invoice; the credit is raised by the core.

## 10. When an instrument is already out of service

Bookings on an instrument that goes out of service are cancelled automatically and the
affected users are notified.

The facility does not automatically rebook you onto another instrument or another slot,
because only you know what your experiment can tolerate — whether a different objective is
acceptable, whether the samples will keep, whether a week's delay matters.

## 11. Teaching and maintenance blocks

Both appear in the calendar as ordinary bookings, so what you see is what is actually
available. Maintenance windows are published at the start of each quarter; teaching blocks
are placed at the start of each term.

Neither can be booked over. A request that would overlap one is refused at the point of
booking with the reason given, rather than accepted and cancelled later.
