# Supplies, Stockroom and Consumables Policy

Owner: Core Facility Operations · Reviewed twice yearly

## 1. What the stockroom is for

The cores hold a small stock of the consumables their instruments depend on, so that work
is not stopped by a missing item with a two-week lead time. It is a buffer, not a shop, and
it is deliberately narrow: items that only one lab uses are that lab's to buy.

## 2. Two categories

**Facility-supplied** items are included in the instrument's hourly rate and are not
charged separately: immersion oil, lens tissue, standard slides, gloves at the bench,
buffer for routine washes.

**Chargeable** items are issued against your account code at the published unit price:
sequencing flow cells and adapters, LC-MS grade solvents, low-bind consumables, calibration
standards, specialist reagents.

The instrument catalogue records which category each item falls into. Where a user is
unsure, ask before using — an item taken from the wrong shelf still generates a charge.

## 3. Getting an item

| Situation | Route |
|---|---|
| Routine quantity, in stock | Take it and scan the barcode at the point of issue |
| Beyond routine quantity | Raise a request at least two weeks ahead |
| Made-to-order item | Raise a request; lead time is quoted per item |
| Not stocked | Buy it through your own department |

Scanning at issue is what creates the charge and decrements the stock count. An item taken
without scanning is not free; it produces a discrepancy at the next stock check, which is
investigated and usually resolved by charging the lab that used the instrument at that time.

## 4. Substitution

Do not substitute your own consumable for a facility-supplied one without checking with
staff. This is not territorial: substituting a different specification is the single most
common cause of results that cannot be compared across sessions, and it is invisible in the
data.

The specifications that matter most are recorded in the consumable standards documents —
refractive index for immersion oil, grade for solvents, binding characteristics for tubes.
Where your work needs a different specification, tell the core and it will be recorded
against your project so that later comparisons account for it.

## 5. Storage and expiry

Items with an opened-date field must have it completed at the moment of opening. An undated
open container is discarded at the next audit without notice, because there is no way to
know whether it is usable.

Expiry is enforced on the shelf, not at the point of use. Staff remove expired stock during
audits; a user finding an expired item should not use it and should tell staff.

Cold-chain items are logged in and out of the relevant storage. Breaking the cold chain
without recording it invalidates the item for everyone who uses it afterwards.

## 6. Reordering

Stock levels are monitored automatically and a reorder is triggered at the item's threshold.
Thresholds are set from consumption history and reviewed quarterly.

A stock-out is recorded and reported. A repeated stock-out on the same item is a threshold
problem rather than a supply problem, and is corrected by raising the threshold.

## 7. Charging and disputes

Consumable charges appear as lines on the monthly invoice, separate from instrument time,
each naming the item, the quantity and the date of issue.

A consumable charge is disputed through the ordinary invoice dispute route. The evidence is
the issue scan: who scanned it, when, and against which account code.

## 8. Returns

Unopened items in original packaging may be returned within seven days and the charge is
reversed. Opened items cannot be returned, and cold-chain items cannot be returned at all
once they have left the storage they were issued from.

## 9. Waste

Consumable waste is segregated at the bench, by the person who generated it. A stream that
arrives at the waste point mixed is rejected and comes back to the lab that sent it,
because separating a mixed stream afterwards is more hazardous than doing it correctly
first.

Sharps containers are replaced at three-quarters full, not when they will no longer close.
