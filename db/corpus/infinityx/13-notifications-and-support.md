# Notifications, Technical Issues and Support Standard

Owner: Core Facility Operations · Reviewed twice yearly

## 1. Getting help — where to start

| Your question is about | Ask |
|---|---|
| An instrument, a booking, access or a charge on it | The core that owns the instrument |
| Your account, a lab membership, an account code | Core administration |
| An invoice line you believe is wrong | The dispute route in the billing policy |
| Experimental design | Your PI — the cores advise on what an instrument can do, not on what your experiment should be |
| Something broken right now | Raise a technical issue, immediately |

The cores are asked most often about things that are already answered in a policy document,
which is why the assistant exists — but a question the documents do not answer is always
worth asking a person.

## 2. Technical issues

A technical issue is anything preventing work: an instrument fault, a software failure, a
door that will not open, a freezer alarm.

Raise it the same day, through the system rather than in a corridor. A fault described to a
staff member in passing does not reach the person who fixes it and does not appear in the
instrument's downtime record — which means it does not count towards the replacement case
for an instrument that keeps failing.

### Severity

| Severity | Meaning | Response target |
|---|---|---|
| Critical | Instrument down, or a safety risk | Same day |
| High | Work degraded, a workaround exists | 1 working day |
| Normal | Inconvenience, no immediate impact | 3 working days |
| Low | Cosmetic, or an improvement request | Best effort |

The person raising an issue proposes the severity; core staff may adjust it, and will say
so. Everything is Critical to the person blocked by it, and staff triage on facility-wide
impact rather than on who reported it.

## 3. Notifications you receive

Automatically, without opting in:

- Booking confirmations, changes and cancellations
- A reminder before a session, at the interval you choose
- Cancellation of a booking because an instrument went out of service
- Service request status changes
- Sample disposal notices, at fourteen days and three days
- Invoice issued, and a reminder before the review period ends
- Budget thresholds at 80% and 100%, to the Group Leader
- Certification expiry, at sixty days and fourteen days

You can change the channel and the timing. You cannot switch off the ones that precede an
irreversible act — disposal and invoice commitment — because the facility will not destroy
material or post a charge against someone who was never told.

## 4. Announcements

Announcements carry facility-wide news: maintenance windows, closures, rate changes, new
instruments, changes to these policies.

They appear on sign-in and are retained for ninety days. Announcements that affect charging
or access are also emailed, because an announcement nobody reads is not notice.

## 5. Escalation

If a technical issue misses its response target, or you disagree with how one was resolved,
escalate to the core manager. If the answer is still wrong, escalate to the facility
directorate.

Escalating is not a complaint about the person who handled it, and the facility does not
treat it as one. The response targets exist to be measured against.

## 6. Support hours

Core staff are on site 09:00 to 17:00 on weekdays. Outside those hours the facility is
accessible to users with out-of-hours authorisation, and issues raised are picked up the
next working day.

There is no on-call rota for user support. Where an out-of-hours fault risks a sample or the
building, the institution's estates emergency number applies rather than the facility's.

## 7. Closures

Planned closures are announced at least fourteen days ahead and bookings in the affected
period are cancelled with notice.

Unplanned closures — power, water, environmental — are announced as soon as the facility
knows. Bookings are cancelled and charges removed. The facility does not automatically
rebook, because only you know what your experiment can tolerate.

## 8. What good looks like when you report something

Give the instrument, the time, what you were doing, and what happened. A screenshot of an
error is worth more than a description of it. If you tried something that changed the
behaviour, say what.

Do not attempt a repair. Do not restart an instrument that has thrown a hardware error
until staff have seen it, because the error state is often the most useful diagnostic
available and restarting discards it.
